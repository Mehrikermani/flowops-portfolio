Automated Operations Reporting

A scheduled reporting approach for identity and IT operations, combining Microsoft Graph data collection, structured processing, readable reports, and conditional Slack notifications.

Azure AutomationMicrosoft GraphPowerShellLogic AppsSlackJSON / HTML

The challenge

Routine checks of identity changes and operational jobs can require repeated manual exports, formatting, and follow-up. Unstructured notifications also make it harder to distinguish meaningful activity from empty reports.

Manual reporting

Recurring collection and formatting takes time away from higher-value IT work.

Inconsistent visibility

Stakeholders need understandable summaries of processed items and exceptions.

Alert fatigue

Notifications should be relevant, not triggered when there is nothing to report.

Solution architecture

Scheduled automation retrieves relevant operational data, transforms it into structured output, and routes summaries to the appropriate channels.

ScheduleRecurring trigger
Azure AutomationRun PowerShell

This is a simplified public architecture showing reusable reporting patterns. It does not expose organization-specific filters, tenant configuration, internal reports, or confidential data.

Implementation highlights

  • Scheduled execution: Run repeatable checks without manual intervention.
  • Scoped Graph queries: Retrieve relevant identity and audit information using appropriate permissions and filters.
  • Structured output: Return JSON containing records, counts, and operational details for downstream processing.
  • Readable reporting: Transform structured records into HTML summaries for IT and relevant stakeholders.
  • Conditional alerts: Avoid Slack notifications when no meaningful records or exceptions are present.
  • Exception visibility: Separate successful actions from skipped or failed items to support troubleshooting.

Engineering approach

The design separates data retrieval, business logic, report formatting, and notification delivery. This makes each stage easier to test, maintain, and adapt to new operational reporting needs.

Related automation experience includes monthly account-disable audit reporting and structured onboarding execution reports. The case study illustrates those patterns rather than claiming one universal production deployment.
